# Pitch: [Working Title] — a Many-Worlds CYOA

## The Hook

You make one choice too many, and suddenly you're not home anymore. You're in a branch reality that's almost right — except your dog's a cat, your job doesn't exist, and everyone insists you've always lived here. This is a Choose Your Own Adventure game about trying to find your way back to the timeline you started in, one decision at a time, while reality gets weirder the further you stray.

## What Makes It Interesting

- **Choices actually echo.** Pick something early, and it doesn't just fork the next scene — it resurfaces 2-3 times later in the story, in ways you'll recognize. It's the one mechanic the whole game is built around: proof that your choices *mattered*, not just that they branched.
- **A story that keeps score.** At the end of each run, a "memory" screen replays the choices you made and what they actually caused, plus a score/tier based on how closely you stuck to the path home. It reframes the whole run as a story about *you*, not just a log of taps.
- **Three ways a run can end.** Make it home, choose to stay in the branch you landed in, or drift into limbo — stuck somewhere you don't quite belong. No single "win" ending; the taxonomy is the payoff for playing.
- **Escalating absurdity (coming later).** The full vision has a deviation meter: the farther you stray from home, the stranger and more surreal the reality gets, gating which content the game draws from. That system is deferred past v1, but the DNA is already there — v1 ships comedic, absurd hard-fail choices (dark-humor gotchas, not fair warnings) that hint at where the escalation is headed.

## Why It Exists

This is a first iOS app, full stop — not a business. The actual goal is to walk the entire path from idea to a real submission on the App Store, on-device only, no backend, no network calls. If it's worth putting in front of strangers, it'll list at $0.99, but that's a nod to "this is a real, finished thing," not a revenue play.

## Scope, On Purpose

v1 is intentionally lean and linear: choice echo, alignment scoring, and the three-way ending taxonomy are the whole game. The richer systems — the live deviation meter, anchor points, a spendable "certainty" resource, earnable rewinds — are all real ideas, just saved for a v1.1 update, so the very first ship stays small enough to actually finish.
