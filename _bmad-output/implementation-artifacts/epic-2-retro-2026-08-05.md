## What Went Well

- **The code-review process consistently earned its keep.** Real, ship-blocking bugs were found on nearly every pass — not stylistic nitpicks. Two standouts: Story 2.3's review disproved an "accepted, not device-verified" assumption (multi-touch card-cancellation was actually structurally unreachable, not just untested), and Story 2.4's second review pass caught a stale `RunProgressObserver`-per-view duplication before it became an inconsistent pattern baked into two places.
- **`RunSnapshot`'s schema extended cleanly, twice, without breaking old saves.** Story 2.9 added `visitedArrivalNodeIds` and Story 2.10 added `visitedNodeIds`, both with backward-compatible decode for pre-existing snapshots — AD-4's original four-field design absorbed two genuine schema extensions with no migration drama.
- **When the user hit a bug directly during their own manual verification, the team fixed it immediately rather than deferring it** (project-context.md's "fix broken windows now" policy, applied repeatedly: Story 2.4's two post-review Home-label bugs, Story 2.8's Reduce-Motion regression, Story 2.9's four rounds of swipe-reliability bugs). Nothing shipped with a known, freshly-discovered player-facing bug left open.
- **A genuine architecture course-correction (the AD-5 `.fullScreenCover` amendment) happened cleanly mid-story** when Story 2.2's gesture conflict couldn't be solved with more UIKit patching — the team escalated to an architect-level design call instead of continuing to fight the platform, and the fix removed the whole conflict class rather than suppressing one symptom.
- **Deferred/open questions from earlier in the epic all got closed by epic end**, not carried forward as debt: the interstitial run-options UX question (discussed, resolved as no-code-change), the "no single leave+clear action" gap (resolved as Story 2.13), and the post-relaunch `goBack()` gap (resolved as Story 2.10).
- **The iOS 26 popover-vs-action-sheet investigation (Story 2.12) modeled good debugging discipline**: a first hypothesis was tried, disproven by the user's own re-test, and the team went to primary sources (WWDC session, Apple Developer Forums) rather than guessing again — landing on a confirmed, cited root cause.

## What Was Challenging

- **SwiftUI gesture arbitration was the single biggest recurring source of bugs in the epic.** Five separate rounds of user-reported Simulator bugs (Stories 2.2, 2.3, 2.8, 2.9) all trace back to the same underlying fact: SwiftUI's own `.gesture`/`.highPriorityGesture` priority system has no authority over a UIKit-backed recognizer (`NavigationStack`'s `interactivePopGestureRecognizer`, `ScrollView`'s `UIPanGestureRecognizer`). This is now written up as its own section in `project-context.md` (see Action Items) so future gesture-touching work starts from documented precedent instead of rediscovering it live.
- **This devcontainer's verification ceiling kept getting hit.** Beyond Story 2.1's already-known blind spot, three more categories of devcontainer-invisible bugs surfaced in Epic 2: a `UserDefaults.standard` persistence-semantics difference (Story 2.4), SwiftUI environment-propagation through `.fullScreenCover` closures (Story 2.7), and modifier-chain color-override order (Story 2.8's invisible-caption bug). Every one of these passed every check available in this devcontainer and was only caught by the user's own build.
- **Story 2.2 absorbed far more than its own scope** — what was specced as straightforward page-navigation wiring turned into a full navigation-architecture rewrite (the AD-5 amendment) discovered only through five rounds of live-device debugging after the story was already in `review`. This was flagged explicitly in that story's own Review Findings as a process/estimation note, not something to re-litigate with blame — but it's worth naming directly here since it's the clearest single example of "a story's real scope wasn't knowable until a human tried it on a real device."
- **Spec text repeatedly lagged discovered reality**, needing explicit RESOLVED CONFLICT banners to reconcile `epics.md`/`ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md` against what actually shipped (Stories 2.1, 2.3, 2.9, 2.12). Each was caught and resolved before the story closed, but it's a recurring tax, not a one-off.

## Key Insights

1. **Gesture-heavy UI work in this codebase should be treated as inherently higher-risk than other SwiftUI work**, on the same footing project-context.md already gives `Package.swift` edits and deletions/renames — not because the code is hard to write, but because its correctness is fundamentally unverifiable in this devcontainer and empirically has needed multiple live-device rounds every time it's been touched.
2. **"Passed `swift test`/`swiftc -parse`" is a necessary but weak signal for anything touching persistence semantics, environment injection, or view-modifier chaining** — three specific categories, now all documented with real Epic 2 examples, where this devcontainer's checks passed clean on genuinely broken code.
3. **The team's discipline around fixing discovered bugs immediately (rather than deferring) kept technical debt from accumulating silently** — worth explicitly continuing into Epic 3, especially since Epic 3's Ending/Memory work will be the first time `RunSnapshot`'s clear-on-ending behavior (built in Story 2.4, exercised throughout Epic 2) gets used for its intended real purpose.
